ARTICLE 1 - KEY DEFINITIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1.1 "Juvenile Policy" - A life insurance policy issued on the
    life of a minor (under age 18) with ownership held by a
    parent, guardian, or other adult.

1.2 "Ownership Transfer" - The automatic or elected transfer
    of policy ownership to the insured upon reaching the age
    of majority (18) or as elected by current owner.

1.3 "Education Income Phase" - Policy years 14-17 (insured
    ages 18-21), during which the policy is designed to
    provide supplemental income for college expenses.

1.4 "Payor Benefit" - A rider feature that waives premium
    if the owner/payor dies or becomes disabled, keeping the
    juvenile policy in force.

1.5 "Guaranteed Insurability Option (GIO)" - The insured's
    guaranteed right to purchase additional life insurance
    at specified future dates without evidence of insurability.

1.6 "Floor Rate" - The minimum indexed interest rate of 0%,
    protecting against market loss.

ARTICLE 3 - HOW INDEX CREDITING WORKS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
3.1 Annual point-to-point crediting:
    Credit = MAX(0%, MIN(Cap, Index% Change × Participation%))

3.2 LONG-TERM COMPOUNDING BENEFIT: Juvenile policies benefit
    uniquely from tax-deferred compound growth over 14+ years
    before any withdrawals are anticipated. Even moderate
    annual credits (4-7%) compound substantially over this
    horizon.

3.3 ZERO FLOOR GUARANTEE: The insured's account will never
    lose value due to negative index performance, providing
    principal protection during market downturns that may
    occur before funds are needed for education.

3.4 EXAMPLE (14-year growth projection at 6% average annual
    credit): $2,400/year × 14 years with 6% avg credit
    produces an estimated account value of approximately
    $52,000-$58,000 (non-guaranteed; illustrated only).

8.4 INSURED RIGHTS AFTER TRANSFER: Upon receiving ownership,

8.5 INCOME RIGHTS WHILE MINOR: Withdrawals and loans by
